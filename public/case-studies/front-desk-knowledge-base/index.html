02 · WHAT WE BUILT

A front desk playbook the clinic owns

Built from their own documents, not a template.
  • 35 protocol pages rewritten from the clinic's own files: scheduling, insurance, charges, recall, triage, the daily checklist. We read every tab, pulled the rules out of side notes and stale copies, and checked each one with the front office manager before it went in.
  • 4 decision tools for the questions anyone at the desk can get wrong, new or experienced: which doctor can see this patient, this year's price, collect or bill, how urgent the call is. Not a chatbot. Each tool asks the questions in order and cannot skip the exception, and each one was tested against real situations before the desk saw it.
  • The managers edit it themselves. Two named people can change any page in the app, with history and restore, built so an edit can never break a page. No developer needed.
  • Locked to clinic accounts. Staff sign in with their clinic email. No patient information inside, by design.
  • Report a gap from any page. Staff tell us what is missing or wrong, and we fix it, usually by the next business morning.
The clinic's part

Their existing documents, shared as they were.

A few short calls with the office manager to check the rules.

Nothing to install: it runs on a free hosting tier in the clinic’s own account. The clinic owns the app and every page in it. After launch, support and updates run on a monthly plan, which is how the clinic keeps it current.

Our part

About two weeks of work between a clinic’s files and a live app: reading all of it, sorting what is current from what is stale, writing pages a new hire can follow on a live call, and deciding which four questions deserve a tool. The first one took longer, because the structure underneath had to be built; that part is done.

The pages are the clinic's own. The structure underneath is the one we perfected on a working front desk, and it is what we build for every clinic. Used every day since August 2026.

How we got the numbers. Hours: about 4 questions a day that used to take 10 minutes of digging or someone else's time now take one search. That is about 200 hours a year, roughly $4,500 in pay. Dollars: add shorter training. The clinic said a new hire takes about 6 months to learn the job; cut that in half for 2 or 3 new hires a year and that is another $8,000 to $12,000. Both are estimates from the clinic's own numbers, kept on the low side.
