What the money buys

Three products, and what each one actually does

Different practices bite on different problems. Two of these are on the price list above. The third is new, and its price is the one thing we cannot print yet.

1 Live today

Front desk knowledge base

For the practice where the hard questions all walk to one desk. Several times a day someone puts a patient on hold and goes to ask. Two people stop working.

We write your insurance rules, prices, and protocols into short situation pages your team can find mid call, in one search box. Your office manager edits them in the app, and every edit is saved and reversible.

  • Decision lookups for the calls that cost money: how soon a caller needs to be seen, which doctor can see them, whether to collect or bill the refraction, and this year's price.
  • Google sign in, restricted to your practice domain. Installs from the browser on the front desk computers, no admin rights.
  • Pages already opened keep working if the internet drops.
  • One click for staff to flag a page that is wrong.

$2,500 setup, $299 a month for the front desk tier

Patient data: none. It holds your rules, not your charts. Zero patient records live in it, so there is nothing in it to breach. Recall is the product where patient contact details are involved, and that one has its own posture below.

2 New

Recall done for you

For the practice where recall is a monthly job sitting on the same checklist as the daily work, so it is the first thing to slip when the phones are busy.

We run your monthly recall for you, using your own recall protocol and your own systems. At an eight provider practice that is 8 separate call and postcard runs, plus the scleral letters, plus the medical letters across about 10 reason codes, plus the update run that has to come last.

  • The run happens on a schedule instead of when someone finds an afternoon.
  • The call list arrives ready to work, in priority order, with the answers to the questions patients ask when they call back.
  • A monthly report: who was contacted, by which channel, and who booked.
  • The update step gets done, every time, in the right order, so the same patients are not pulled again next month.

Priced per practice, on the call. We have not set a price yet, and we will not print a guess

Patient data: yes, contact information. Recall involves patient contact details, so it runs inside your own accounts under a signed BAA. We never touch charts. Different product, different posture, and we say it per product rather than once for the whole site.

3 Included in tier 3

Practice operations automation

For the owner or office manager whose week is eaten by time off requests, payroll prep, and pulling numbers nobody has time to pull.

Time off and payroll, automated and scoped to your practice. At the practice we built it for it returns 60 to 100 admin hours a year. It comes with a quarterly practice snapshot and monthly working time with both of us.

  • Requests, approvals, and balances stop living in text messages and a spreadsheet.
  • Payroll prep comes out of the same records, so the numbers agree.
  • A quarterly snapshot of the practice, written for the owner, not exported from a dashboard.

$4,500 setup, $350 a month as part of knowledge base plus operations

Patient data: none. This one is staff records and practice operations. It never touches patient information at all.

The two questions everyone asks

Patient data, and your practice management system

Is this HIPAA trouble

The answer is per product, because the products are different. Anyone giving you one answer for a whole catalogue has not thought about it.

Knowledge base: it holds rules, prices, and protocols. Zero patient records. There is nothing in it to breach.

Recall done for you: this one does involve patient contact information. It runs inside your own accounts, under a signed BAA, with a limited login you create and can revoke. We never touch charts.

Operations: staff records and practice operations only. No patient information at all.

Does it connect to our practice management system

No, and that is on purpose. It does not matter which system you run.

Hosted practice management systems rarely hand an outside vendor direct database access, so a promised sync is the kind of thing that falls over in week three. We would rather build on something that does not depend on it.

For the knowledge base there is nothing to connect: it holds your rules, not your records. For recall, we work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke on any day you like.
