# Round 2 brief: the visual, demo led direction

Read `BRIEF.md` first. It still governs every fact, price, product definition, voice rule,
brand token and technical constraint. This file only says what changed for prototypes 11 to 17.

Round 1 produced ten directions. Alec picked three: **01 the product grid**, **05 demo first**,
and **08 dark operator**. What he liked, in his words: the dynamic demos and the graphic visual
elements, without overloading on text. Round 2 explores that lane properly, and adds two sections
round 1 did not have.

---

## 1. The four changes

### A. Less prose. Much less.

Round 1 pages argue in paragraphs. These should argue in **things you can look at and drive**.

- **Hard budget: no more than about 60 words of running prose per section.** Not counting labels,
  table cells, list items, or copy inside a depiction.
- No paragraph longer than three sentences. Most should be one.
- If a point can be made by a diagram, a working panel, or a number, make it that way instead.
- This is a reduction in **prose**, not in substance. Every fact required by BRIEF.md section 7
  still has to be on the page. Move it into the visual, the caption, or the table.

### B. Every product gets something that moves.

Each of the three products must have an element the visitor can **actually operate**, not a
picture of software. A static mock up is a round 1 answer.

- Front desk knowledge base: a search box that really filters and really opens an answer.
- Recall done for you: something that shows the shape of the run. A month view, a provider list
  that expands, a run sequence that steps, a call list that can be worked.
- Practice operations automation: a request that can be submitted and approved, a calendar that
  fills, a payroll line that resolves.

Vanilla JS, no dependencies, keyboard operable, and the page must still be complete and readable
if the script never runs. Build the content in the markup and enhance it, never generate it.

### C. Stop wrapping everything in bubbles. This is Alec's note and it is not optional.

The round 1 pages put nearly everything inside a frosted glass pane, and panes ended up nested
inside panes. It reads as busy and it flattens the hierarchy, because when everything is elevated
nothing is.

- **The hero gets at most ONE contained element.** The headline, the sub and the buttons sit
  directly on the background. The one pane, if you use it, is the product depiction.
- **Never nest a pane inside a pane.** A card inside a card is banned outright.
- **Across the whole page, at most two bubble wrapped regions.** Everything else earns its
  separation from a full bleed band, a background shift, a hairline rule, a change of measure, or
  plain open space.
- Whitespace and typography do the work that borders were doing. This will feel emptier than round
  one. That is the point.

### D. Two new sections, required on all seven.

**Testimonials.** See section 2. Quote rules are strict.

**Custom builds.** See section 3. We do more than the three products and the site does not say so.

## 2. Testimonials: use only these quotes, verbatim

These are real lines from real client emails at the 27 person eyecare practice. **Do not write a
new quote. Do not paraphrase, extend, punch up, or combine these. Do not invent a name.** If a
quote does not fit the layout, use a shorter one from this list or use fewer quotes.

Attribute **by role only**, exactly as written in the Attribution column. Do not print the practice
name, the people's names, or the city. Permission to do that has not been asked for yet.

| Quote, verbatim | Attribution | Context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "You are awesome! Thank you so much for your help!" | Office manager | After a round of fixes to the time off system |
| "Thanks for the fixes on the PTO!" | Office manager | Time off system |
| "Thank you for all of your help on this project." | Office manager | Time off system, at handover |
| "That looks good, exactly what Mary needs to process." | Practice administrator | On the payroll output |
| "I think the biggest issue will be getting in the habit of using the tool." | Office manager | Two weeks after the knowledge base went live |

**Use that last one.** It is the most valuable quote here and it is not praise. A page that prints
a client saying the hard part is adoption, and then answers it, is more credible than any rave.
The answer is already true and already house voice: getting people to use it is a separate job and
we treat it that way, which is what the feedback button and the same day fixes in the first two
weeks are for. Build that as a quote and response pair.

**You may also state these as fact, because they are documented, but not as quotes:**

- Staff report problems from inside the app rather than by email. The first batch of four issues
  was fixed and live the same day.
- The front office manager sends new pages to add. One went live from a screenshot the same week.
- The practice's own team files the issues. The tool is in daily use at the front desk.

**Design the section so it survives a better quote.** Once permission and a real endorsement land,
it should be a copy swap and not a rebuild. Do not build a layout that only works with five short
lines, and do not build one that needs a photograph.

## 3. Custom builds: the fourth thing we sell

The three products are what we have packaged. They are not everything we do, and the site currently
hides that. Add a section saying we also build to order.

Use these real engagements. Do not invent others and do not invent numbers.

| Build | What it was | The line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business coaching practice | A full operating system: software agents handling inbox, scheduling, voice note capture, meeting notes, and calendar to invoice | 5 agents running daily operations |
| Construction company, Japan | A full site rebuild across six pages, search console set up, and a corrected business profile, now on an upkeep retainer | Complete digital rebuild |
| Language test prep company | AI worked into curriculum drafting, formatting, review cycles, and process automation | 80% of the manual work cut |

Two things to get right:

1. **Frame it as capability, not as a menu.** The three products are the front door. Custom is what
   happens when a practice has a problem that is not one of the three. Keep it compact. It must not
   compete with the products for attention.
2. **No price.** Custom work is scoped on the call. Say that.

Positioning line you may use: the products exist because we built them for one practice first. If
the thing in your way is not one of the three, that is the conversation.
