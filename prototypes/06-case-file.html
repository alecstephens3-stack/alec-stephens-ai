What we found

None of this arrived in a survey. It is what happens on the floor between the first patient and the last one, written the way we saw it.

  1. F-01

    The hard questions all route through one or two people

    Several times a day someone puts a patient on hold and walks to the office manager's desk. Two people stop working. The answer existed. It was not findable while a patient waited on the line.

    Answered by entry 01
  2. F-02

    Nobody was certain which price was current

    Price lists stacked inside one spreadsheet, each of them correct at some point in the past. When the answer is unclear the front desk guesses. The guess turns up later as an uncollected charge or a surprise balance at checkout.

    Answered by entry 01
  3. F-03

    Training restarted from nothing with every hire

    Turnover at a front desk is normal. Losing months of training each time is not. The knowledge was real. It lived in a few people's heads and in a binder nobody had updated since the last insurance change.

    Answered by entry 01
  4. F-04

    The monthly recall sat on the same list as the daily work

    So it is the first thing to slip when the phones are busy. The run itself is fragile too. One step is a checkbox that has to be left unchecked on one run and checked on another, and getting it backwards either contacts patients twice or drops them entirely.

    Answered by entry 03
  5. F-05

    Time off approvals lived in messages and memory

    Requests arrived three different ways. Coverage was checked by asking around the floor. Payroll picked up whatever survived the trip, and the office manager carried the rest.

    Answered by entry 02

Entry 01 Live at the front desk

Front desk knowledge base

Built first because it was the finding that interrupted the most people per day. It went live on day 30 and it is in daily use.

What it is

A searchable knowledge base holding the insurance rules, prices, and protocols the front desk was keeping in their heads. Short situation pages the team can find mid call, from one search box.

Who it is for

The people answering the phone, and the office manager who up to now has been the search engine. Independent practices with one to three locations, where the answer usually lives with whoever has been there longest.

  • 76 front desk situations mapped from the questions staff actually field, written by us from the practice's own material.
  • 4 decision lookups for the calls that cost money: how soon a caller needs to be seen, which doctor can see them, whether to collect or bill the refraction, and this year's price.
  • The office manager edits it in the app. Every edit is saved, and every edit is reversible.
  • Google sign in, restricted to the practice domain. Nobody outside it can open the file.
  • Installs from the browser on the front desk computers, with no admin rights. Pages already opened keep working if the internet drops.
  • One click for staff to flag a page that is wrong, from inside the page, while they are still looking at it.
  • Holds no patient records. It holds the practice's rules, not its charts.

Entry 03 In build now

Recall done for you

We run the practice's monthly patient recall for them, using the practice's own recall protocol and its own systems. This is the entry that is open. Read Exhibit B first and the rest of this page will make sense.

What it is

We take the run off the practice's monthly checklist and do it ourselves, to the practice's own written protocol, inside the practice's own systems. The scleral letters, the medical letters, every per provider call list and postcard run, then the update step last, in that order, on a fixed date every month.

Who it is for

Practices where the recall run is real work that a real person has to find time for, and where the month it gets skipped is the month nobody notices until the schedule thins out. You keep the protocol. We run it.

What the practice gets

  • The run happens on a schedule instead of when someone finds an afternoon.
  • The call list arrives ready to work, in priority order, with the answers to the questions patients ask when they call back.
  • A monthly report: who was contacted, by which channel, and who booked.
  • The update step gets done, every time, in the right order, by the people whose only job that month is this run.

Limits, entered into the record

It does not plug into the practice management system. We work from a list the practice's own team runs, or inside their system with a limited login the practice creates and can revoke at any time.

Recall does involve patient contact information. Unlike the knowledge base, this is not a zero patient data product. It runs inside the practice's own accounts under a signed BAA, and we never touch charts.

We will not quote you a recovery rate. We do not have one to quote. What is being sold here is the mechanism and the reliability: that the run happens, and that the update step is done last and done right.

The price is not set yet. Recall is priced per practice, on the call, or folded into tier 3. We would rather say that than print a number we would have to walk back.

Two questions, answered on the record

Every owner asks these two before they ask anything else. Here they are answered once, in writing, with the awkward parts left in.

Is this HIPAA trouble

The honest answer is that it depends on which product, so we state the posture per product rather than making one comfortable claim about the whole file. We sign a BAA before we start either way.

Entry 01, front desk knowledge base

Holds 0 patient records. It holds your rules: insurance policy, pricing, scheduling protocol, procedure. There are no charts in it, so there is nothing in it to breach.

Entry 02, practice operations

Staff time off and payroll information, scoped to your practice and run inside your own accounts. No patient records.

Entry 03, recall done for you

This one does involve patient contact information. It runs inside your own accounts under a signed BAA, and we never touch charts. We are not going to repeat the knowledge base line across a product where it is not true.

Does this connect to our practice management system

No, and that is on purpose. The hosted practice management systems used in this field do not hand an outside vendor database access. Any vendor promising you a nightly sync into a hosted system is either overselling or has not checked yet.

So the knowledge base does not care which system you run. And for recall we work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke. The consequence is that we do not become a dependency you cannot switch off.

Appended to the record

What happens if you cancel

It is month to month with no term. You keep every page. You can export any time, as plain files you can open without us. The content is your practice's rules. We wrote them down. We do not own them.
