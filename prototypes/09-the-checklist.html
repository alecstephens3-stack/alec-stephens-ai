The three things the checklist points at.

Each one is a finished build, not a platform we hand you empty. Here is what each is, who it is for, and what it looks like in use.

1 Live today

Front desk knowledge base

For the practice where the hard questions all go through one or two people.

Your insurance rules, prices, and protocols written up as short situation pages the team can find mid call. One search box. It is live in a 27 person practice with 6 to 7 doctors at one location, where we mapped 76 front desk situations and built 4 decision lookups.

  • Decision lookups for the calls that cost money: how soon a caller needs to be seen, which doctor can see them, collect or bill the refraction, and this year's price.
  • Your office manager edits it in the app. Every edit is saved, so a wrong change is one click back.
  • Google sign in, restricted to your practice domain. Installs from the browser on the front desk computers with no admin rights.
  • Pages already opened keep working if the internet drops, and staff flag a wrong page in one click.

Data posture. It holds your rules, not your charts. 0 patient records are in it, so there is nothing in it to breach. We sign a BAA before we start either way.

2 The new one

Recall done for you

For the practice whose recall is a monthly job that keeps losing to the phones.

We run your monthly patient recall for you, using your own recall protocol and your own systems. The run happens on a schedule instead of when someone finds an afternoon, and the update step gets done every time, in the right order.

  • Scleral letters, the medical recall letters across about 10 reason codes, and the calls list and routine postcards run once per doctor.
  • The call list arrives ready to work, in priority order, with the answers to the questions patients ask when they call back.
  • A monthly report: who was contacted, by which channel, and who booked.
  • We sell you the mechanism and the reliability. We have no recovery rate to quote at you, so we will not.

Data posture, and it is different from the knowledge base. Recall does involve patient contact information. It runs inside your own accounts under a signed BAA, and we never touch charts. We work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke.

3 The quiet one

Practice operations automation

For the owner or office manager still doing admin by hand on a weekend.

Time off and payroll, automated and scoped to your practice. It returned 60 to 100 admin hours a year at the practice we built it for. It comes with a quarterly practice snapshot and monthly working time with both founders.

  • Time off requests, balances, and approvals stop living on paper and in one person's spreadsheet.
  • The pay run is prepared from the same data instead of assembled from several places every cycle.
  • A quarterly snapshot of the practice, and monthly working time with both of us.

Data posture. This is staff scheduling and hours, not charts. It is scoped to your practice and runs alongside the knowledge base in tier 3.

The questions every owner asks, answered before you ask them.

Is this HIPAA trouble

The answer is per product, because the posture is per product. The knowledge base holds your rules, not your charts: insurance policies, pricing, scheduling protocols, procedures. There are 0 patient records in it, so there is nothing in it to breach.

Recall is different and we will not blur it. Recall does involve patient contact information, so it runs inside your own accounts under a signed BAA, and we never touch charts. The operations work is staff schedules and hours. We sign the BAA before we start, whichever one you buy.

Does it connect to our practice management system

No, and that is on purpose. It does not connect, so it does not matter which one you run. Most hosted practice management systems do not give an outside vendor database access. Any vendor promising a nightly sync into a hosted system is either overselling or has not checked yet.

For recall we say the same thing plainly: we work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke at any time.

Who are we actually hiring

Two people, and you work with both of us rather than an account manager. We are based in Asia, so we are not at a desk during your morning rush. Urgent issues get answered by the next business morning, US time, and the escalation path goes in writing before you sign anything.

We would rather state the limit than imply a promise we cannot keep.
