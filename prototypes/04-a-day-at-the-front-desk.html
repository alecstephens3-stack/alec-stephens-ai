4:00 p.m. Recall, still on the checklist

The monthly run that has been on the checklist since the first

It is not one job. It is the scleral letters. Then the medical recall letters, across about 10 separate reason codes, output as postcards. Then the call list and the routine postcards, once per doctor, individually, which at eight providers is 8 separate runs. Then, only after every call and every postcard is finished, the update run so the same patients are not pulled again next month.

Two things make it fragile. The update step is a checkbox that has to be left unchecked on one run and checked on another, and getting it backwards either double contacts patients or drops them. And it sits on the same checklist as the daily work, so it is the first thing to slip when the phones are busy.

Where does our patient data sit?

The honest answer is different per product, so we say it per product rather than making one comfortable claim across the whole company.

Knowledge base It holds your rules, not your charts: insurance policies, pricing, scheduling protocols, procedures. There are 0 patient records in it, so there is nothing in it to breach.
Recall done for you This one does involve patient contact information. It runs inside your own accounts, under a signed BAA, on lists your team controls. We never touch charts, and access can be revoked by you at any moment.
Operations automation Staff schedules and time off, inside your own accounts. No patient data of any kind.

Does this connect to our practice management system?

No, and that is on purpose. Most hosted practice management systems do not give an outside vendor database access. Any vendor promising you a nightly sync into a hosted system is either overselling or has not checked yet.

So it does not matter which system you run. The knowledge base sits alongside it and answers the questions the software was never going to answer anyway.

For recall, we work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login your practice creates and can revoke. No integration to break, nothing to renegotiate when your vendor changes their terms.

What this costs you Someone on your team runs the list, the same way they do today. That is the trade, and we would rather name it now than have you find it out after we start.
