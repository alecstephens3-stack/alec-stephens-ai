Billing

Refraction: collect or bill

The vision plan covers the refraction only when the visit bills as a routine exam.

  • Routine exam on the vision plan: it goes through the plan and there is nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit: the refraction is not covered. Say so at check in, not at checkout.
  • If the doctor changes the visit type after the exam, the answer changes with it.
Exception

Check the encounter before you take payment. A visit that started routine and ended medical bills medical.

Insurance

Vision plan or medical: which one bills

The reason the patient gives on the phone decides which benefit the visit runs on.

  • Due for glasses or an annual check with no complaint: routine, vision plan.
  • Names a symptom, redness, pain, flashes, a sudden change: medical, even if they also want glasses.
  • Both in one visit: the doctor decides which one bills. Do not promise the patient it will be the plan.
Exception

A patient with a diagnosis already on file who calls for a routine check still bills medical if the doctor addresses it.

Scheduling

Same day or next available

The short list of what cannot wait for the next open routine slot.

  • Sudden flashes, new floaters, a curtain or shadow in the vision: same day, and tell the doctor on duty.
  • Chemical splash or something in the eye: same day, and start them rinsing while they are still on the phone.
  • A red, painful eye in a contact lens wearer: same day.
  • Gradual blur, no pain, no redness: routine, next available.
Exception

If nothing is open, do not send them away. Take the message to the doctor on duty and call back the same day.

Scheduling

Which doctor can see this patient

Not every visit type fits every provider's schedule. Check before you book, not after.

  • Young children and first eye exams go to the providers who take them that week.
  • Specialty fittings and post op follow ups go back to the provider who started the case.
  • Some providers do not take walk in medical visits on their surgery days.
Exception

Book the right provider on a later day instead of filling the wrong slot. A visit that has to be redone costs more than the wait.

Billing

Price lookup: which list is current

One current list. The old copies in the shared drive and the printed sheet in the drawer are not it.

  • These prices are the ones your office manager last approved. If a printed sheet disagrees, this page wins.
  • Self pay and plan prices are different. Ask which one applies before you quote anything.
  • If a price is not on the page, say you will confirm and call back. Do not estimate.
Exception

Prices reset at the start of the plan year, so check the page again when the year turns.

Optical

Contact lens follow up: what is included

Follow ups inside the fitting period are part of the fitting fee. After it closes they are a new charge.

  • A recheck inside the fitting period: no new charge. Book it as a follow up, not a new exam.
  • A new brand or a power change asked for after the fitting closes: new fitting.
  • Still has trials and wants a different lens: ask the doctor before you order anything.
Exception

A patient who never came back is still inside the fitting period until it closes. Do not charge them twice to finish it.

Recall

Recall cards: what to say on a call back

Patients call and ask why they got a card. Answer from the reason code, not from a guess.

  • Routine recall: they are due for their annual exam. Book it.
  • Medical recall: the doctor asked to see them again for something specific. Say the doctor asked, then book it.
  • Scleral lens wearers get their own letter, and those patients usually already know why.
Exception

If the patient says they were already seen, do not argue. Pass it to the office manager so the record is corrected and the cards stop.
