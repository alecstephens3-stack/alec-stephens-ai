What we build

01 The front desk knowledge base

For the practice where the answer exists, but only inside two people, and both of them are already on the phone.

Every insurance rule, price, and protocol your front desk currently keeps in their heads, written up as short situation pages they can find mid call. One search box. We wrote 76 of those pages at the practice this is live in, plus 4 decision lookups for the calls that cost money: how soon a caller needs to be seen, which doctor can see them, whether to collect or bill the refraction, and this year's price.

Your office manager edits it in the app, so a price change is live the moment they save it, and every edit is saved and reversible. Staff sign in with the practice Google account. It installs on the front desk computers from the browser, with no admin rights, and pages already opened keep working if the internet drops. It holds 0 patient records.

$2,500 to set up, $299 a month. Live today in a 27 person practice. Most practices should start one tier up. See the prices below.

02 Recall done for you

For the practice where the monthly recall run keeps sliding into next month, because the phones do not stop.

We run your monthly recall for you, using your recall protocol and your systems. That means the scleral letters, the medical recall letters across about 10 separate reason codes as postcards, and then the calls list and the routine postcards run once per doctor, individually. At an eight provider practice that is 8 separate runs before anyone picks up a phone.

The part that actually breaks is the update step at the end. It is a checkbox that has to be left unchecked on one run and checked on another. Backwards one way and patients get contacted twice. Backwards the other way and they get dropped for the year. It has to run last, after the final call, and it is the step that gets skipped when the day gets away from everyone.

You get the run on a schedule instead of when someone finds a free afternoon, a call list in priority order with the answers to the questions patients ask when they call back, a monthly report of who was contacted, by which channel, and who booked, and the update step done every time, in the right order.

Priced per practice, on the call. We have not set a list price for recall yet, and we are not going to invent one on a landing page. It also folds into the third tier below.

The two things you are about to ask

Where does our patient data sit?

It depends on the product, so here it is per product rather than as one comfortable line across the whole page.

Knowledge base. It holds your rules, not your charts. Insurance policies, prices, scheduling protocols, procedures. There are 0 patient records in it, so there is nothing in it to breach.
Recall done for you. This one does involve patient contact information, so we will not pretend otherwise. It runs inside your own accounts, under a signed BAA, and we never touch charts.
Operations automation. Staff schedules and payroll, in your accounts. No patient information passes through it.

Does it connect to our practice management system?

No, and that is on purpose. Most hosted systems do not give an outside vendor database access. A vendor promising you a nightly sync into one has either not checked or is overselling, and you will find out during implementation rather than on the sales call.

So the knowledge base does not need it. It holds the rules, and the rules do not live in your system anyway. They live in your staff's heads.

For recall, where the work genuinely happens inside your system, we work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login your practice creates and can revoke. That is the honest version. It also means it does not matter which system you run.

Both of us are based in Asia. We are not at a desk during your morning rush. Urgent issues get answered by the next business morning, US time, and we put the escalation path in writing before you sign anything. We would rather state the limit now than imply a promise we cannot keep.
