Billing

Refraction: collect or bill

The vision plan covers the refraction only when the visit bills as a routine exam.

  • Routine exam on the vision plan: the refraction goes through the plan and there is nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit: the refraction is not a covered service. Say so at check in, not at checkout.

Exception

If the doctor changes the visit type after the exam, the answer changes with it. Check the encounter before you take payment.

Insurance

Vision plan or medical: which one bills

The reason the patient gives on the phone decides which benefit the visit runs on.

  • Due for glasses or an annual check with no complaint: routine, vision plan.
  • Names a symptom, red eye, pain, flashes, a sudden change: medical, even if they also want glasses.
  • Both in one visit: the doctor decides which one bills. Do not promise the patient it will be the vision plan.

Exception

A patient with a medical diagnosis already on file who calls for a routine check still bills medical if the doctor addresses it. Say the plan may not apply before they come in.

Scheduling

Same day or next available: phone triage

The short list of what cannot wait for the next open routine slot.

  • Sudden flashes, new floaters, a curtain or a shadow in the vision: same day, and tell the doctor on duty.
  • Chemical splash or something in the eye: same day, and start them rinsing while they are still on the phone.
  • Gradual blur with no pain and no redness: routine, next available.

Exception

If nothing is open, do not send them away. Take the message to the doctor on duty and call the patient back with an answer the same day.

Scheduling

Which doctor can see this patient

Not every visit type fits every provider's schedule. Check before you book, not after.

  • Young children and first eye exams go to the providers who take them. The lookup shows who is taking them this week.
  • Specialty fittings and post op follow ups go back to the provider who started the case.
  • Some providers do not take walk in medical visits on their surgery days.

Exception

If the only opening is with a provider who does not take that visit type, book the right provider on a later day. A visit that has to be redone costs more than the wait.

Optical

Contact lens follow up: what is included

Follow up visits inside the fitting period are part of the fitting fee. After it closes they are a new charge.

  • A recheck inside the fitting period: no new charge. Book it as a follow up, not as a new exam.
  • A new brand or a power change the patient asks for after the fitting closes: new fitting.
  • Still has trials and wants a different lens: ask the doctor before you order anything.

Exception

A patient who never came back for the recheck is still inside the fitting period until it closes. Do not charge them again to finish what they already paid for.

Billing

Price lookup: which list is current

One current list. The old copies in the shared drive and the printed sheet in the drawer are not it.

  • The prices here are the ones your office manager last approved. If a printed sheet disagrees with this page, this page wins.
  • Self pay and plan prices are different. Ask which one applies before you quote anything.
  • If a price is not on the page, say you will confirm and call back. Do not estimate.

Exception

Prices reset at the start of the plan year. A quote given before the reset is not honoured after it, so check the page again when the year turns.

Recall

Recall reason codes: what to say on the call back

Patients call and ask why they got a card. Answer from the code, not from a guess.

  • Routine recall: they are due for their annual exam. Book it.
  • Medical recall: the doctor asked to see them again for something specific. Say the doctor asked, then book it.
  • Scleral lens wearers get their own letter, and those patients usually already know why.

Exception

If the patient says they were already seen, do not argue and do not rebook. Take the note to the office manager so the record gets corrected and the cards stop.

Operations

Time off request: what to tell staff

One route in, one answer out, so nothing gets approved twice.

  • Requests go in through the request form, not to a person in the hallway.
  • Coverage is checked against the schedule before anything is approved.
  • The approved answer goes back to the staff member and to payroll at the same time.

Exception

A verbal yes from anyone is not an approval. If it did not come back through the system, it did not happen.

The two questions every owner asks

Is this HIPAA trouble?

It depends on the product, so we set the posture per product instead of making one claim for everything.

  • Knowledge base: no patient data. It holds your rules, not your charts. 0 patient records, so there is nothing in it to breach.
  • Recall done for you: patient contact information. It runs inside your own accounts under a signed BAA, with a limited login you create and can revoke. We never touch charts.
  • Operations automation: staff time and pay. No patient data at all.

Does it connect to our practice management system?

No, and that is on purpose rather than a gap we are hiding.

  • Most hosted systems do not give an outside vendor database access. Any vendor promising a nightly sync is either overselling or has not checked yet.
  • Because it does not connect, it does not matter which system you run. Nothing about your setup blocks the build.
  • For recall we work from a list your own team runs, or a limited login you create and can revoke in a minute without calling us.
  • What you get back is a build that starts in week one instead of dying in a vendor access request.
