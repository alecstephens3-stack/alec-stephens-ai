What we found at the front desk

Alec spent the last year inside a 27 person eyecare practice, 6 to 7 doctors at one location, in the office, watching people answer the phone. Three things showed up every week.

1.1 The hard questions go through one or two people

Several times a day someone puts a patient on hold and walks to the office manager's desk. Two people stop working. The answer was written down somewhere. Just not anywhere anyone can find it while a patient waits on the line.

1.2 Nobody is certain which price is current

At the practice we built this for, more than one price list lived in the same spreadsheet, and it was not obvious which one was current. When the answer is unclear the front desk guesses. The guess turns up later as an uncollected charge, or as a surprise balance at checkout.

1.3 The monthly jobs slip first

Patient recall sits on the same checklist as the daily work. It runs when someone finds an afternoon, which means some months it does not run. The daily work always wins, because the phone is ringing now.

These are not our observations about eyecare in general. They are what we saw in one building, over a year, and they are the reason each of the three products exists.

The three products, in specification

Each one is set out the same way: what it does, what it does not do, what it costs, and what we need from you. Read the second row first. It is the row most vendors leave out.

2.1 Front desk knowledge base

What it is. A searchable knowledge base holding the insurance rules, prices, and protocols your front desk currently keeps in their heads. Your protocols become short situation pages the team can find mid call, from one search box.

Who it is for. A practice where the same handful of questions interrupt the same one or two people all day, and where a new hire takes months to become useful.

Specification 2.1
What it does
  • Returns a short situation page in a few seconds, from one search box, while the patient is still on the line.
  • Runs 4 decision lookups for the calls that cost money: how soon a caller needs to be seen, which doctor can see them, whether to collect or bill the refraction, and this year's price.
  • Lets your office manager edit any page in the app. Every edit is saved, so a wrong change is one click back.
  • Signs staff in with their practice Google account, restricted to your domain, with editors you name.
  • Installs from the browser on the front desk computers. No admin rights, no ticket to your IT vendor.
  • Keeps working on pages already opened if the internet drops.
  • Gives staff one click to flag a page that is wrong.
What it does not do
  • It holds no patient records. Zero. Your rules, not your charts.
  • It does not connect to your practice management system. See section 5.
  • It does not replace clinical judgment. Clinical pages are a reference, and your doctor signs off before one goes live and again when it changes.
  • It is not an empty system you fill in yourself. We write the pages from the material you already have.
What it costs Tier 1 at $2,500 setup and $299 a month for the front desk. Tier 2 at $3,500 setup and $250 a month for the whole floor, which is where we would start you. Full schedule in section 3.
What we need from you
  • The material you already have, unsorted. Protocols, price sheets, insurance notes, the binder.
  • A few short sessions with the people who answer the phone, scheduled around the practice.
  • One person who owns the content after launch. Usually the office manager.
  • A doctor to sign off on anything clinical.

2.2 Recall done for you

What it is. We run your monthly patient recall for you, using your own recall protocol and your own systems. Not a new process. The one you already wrote down, executed on a schedule.

Who it is for. A practice where recall is real work that competes with the phones, and where the month it gets skipped is invisible until the schedule thins out later.

Here is what the job actually is, taken from one practice's written protocol. Run the scleral letters. Run the medical recall letters across about 10 separate reason codes, output as postcards. Run the calls list and the routine postcards once per doctor, individually, so an eight provider practice is eight separate runs. Then, only after every call and every postcard is finished, go back and run the update step so the same patients are not pulled again next month.

Two things make it fragile. The update step is a checkbox that has to be left unchecked on one run and checked on another, and getting it backwards either contacts patients twice or drops them entirely. And it is a monthly job on the same checklist as the daily work, so it is the first thing to slip.

Specification 2.2
What it does
  • Runs on a schedule instead of when someone finds an afternoon.
  • Delivers the call list ready to work, in priority order, with the answers to the questions patients ask when they call back.
  • Runs the scleral letters, the medical recall across every reason code, and the calls and postcards once per doctor.
  • Runs the update step every time, after the contact runs, so the same patients are not pulled again next month.
  • Sends a monthly report: who was contacted, by which channel, and who booked.
What it does not do
  • It does not plug into your practice management system. We work from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke.
  • It does not put an outside voice on the phone with your patients. The call list goes to your own team, who already know them.
  • We do not quote a recovery rate or a revenue figure. We have not run enough months anywhere to have an honest one, and we would rather sell you the mechanism than a number we made up.
  • We never touch charts.
What it costs Not set. This is the newest of the three, and we price it per practice on the call once we have seen your protocol and how many providers it runs across. It can also be folded into tier 3. We would rather leave this line blank than print a number we would have to walk back.
What we need from you
  • Your written recall protocol, including the reason codes you use.
  • Either a list your team runs each month, or a limited login you create and can revoke at any time.
  • A signed BAA before we start. Recall involves patient contact information, so it runs inside your own accounts. See section 4.
  • One person on your side who takes the call list and works it.

Who it is for. A practice where the office manager's week is eaten by requests, approvals, and the spreadsheet that has to agree with payroll at the end of the month.

Specification 2.3
What it does

  • Gives you a quarterly practice snapshot, so the operations picture is written down somewhere other than one person's memory.
  • Includes monthly working time with both founders, on your work, not a status call.
What it does not do
  • It does not replace your payroll provider or your accountant.
  • It does not touch clinical systems or patient charts.
  • It is not a product you buy off a shelf. It is built against your rules, which means it only makes sense once we know them.
What it costs Included in tier 3: $4,500 setup and $350 a month, with the whole practice knowledge base underneath it.
What we need from you
  • Your time off policy as it is actually applied, including the exceptions nobody wrote down.
  • Access to the accounts the work runs in, created by you and revocable by you.
  • An hour a month from whoever owns the schedule.

3.1 What the monthly is actually for

The writing, not the hosting. Hosting this costs almost nothing. When an insurance rule changes, a doctor's restrictions change, or your prices reset for the year, we update the pages and run the accuracy review. That is our job rather than something the office manager does on a weekend. A knowledge base that goes stale becomes the exact problem it was built to solve.

3.2 The comparison that matters

Compare us to a training platform, not to your practice management software. Trainual and Whale run about $249 to $300 a month at this size and still hand you an empty system. You write every page yourself. We write the pages from what you already have, then hand you the keys.

If you would rather not pay a monthly at all, cancel after launch, keep the exported content, and maintain it yourself. We will tell you the two easiest ways to get search back.

05

Why it does not connect to your practice management system

It does not connect. That is on purpose, and it is the part of this document we would most like you to read twice.

Most hosted practice management systems do not give an outside vendor database access. Any vendor promising you a nightly sync into a hosted system is either overselling or has not checked yet. We checked.

So the knowledge base holds your rules, not your records, and it does not care which system you run. Nothing about the build changes if you switch systems next year. Recall works from a list your own team runs, or inside your system with a limited login you create and can revoke in one click if you ever want us out.

The trade is real and we will state it plainly. You do not get a live feed. What you get instead is a system with no integration to break, no vendor holding your data hostage, and no dependency on a software company deciding to keep an interface open.

If a live feed is the thing you actually want, we are not the right vendor and we will say so on the call rather than after the invoice.
