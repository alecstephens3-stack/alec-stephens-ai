Scheduling · Same day or next available: phone triage

The short list of what cannot wait for the next open routine slot.

  • Same day. Sudden flashes, new floaters, a curtain or a shadow in the vision. Tell the doctor on duty.
  • Same day. Chemical splash, or something in the eye. Start them rinsing while they are still on the phone.
  • Routine. Gradual blur with no pain and no redness.
Exception: if nothing is open, do not send them away. Take the message to the doctor on duty and call back the same day.
Scheduling · Which doctor can see this patient

Not every visit type fits every provider's schedule. Check before you book, not after.

  • Children and first exams. The lookup shows who is taking them this week.
  • Specialty fittings and post op. Back to the provider who started the case.
  • Surgery days. Some providers do not take walk in medical visits on them.
Exception: book the right provider on a later day rather than filling the wrong slot. A visit that has to be redone costs more than the wait.
Billing · Refraction: collect or bill

The vision plan covers the refraction only when the visit bills as a routine exam.

  • Routine on the vision plan. It goes through the plan. Nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit. Not a covered service. Say so at check in, not at checkout.
  • Visit type changed after the exam. The answer changes with it. Check the encounter before you take payment.
Exception: if the visit bills medical, collect the refraction at checkout, and tell the patient at check in so it is not a surprise.
Billing · Price lookup: which list is current

One current list. The old copies in the shared drive and the printed sheet in the drawer are not it.

  • This page wins. If a printed sheet disagrees with it, the sheet is out of date.
  • Self pay and plan prices differ. Ask which one applies before you quote.
  • Not listed? Say you will confirm and call back. Do not estimate.
Exception: prices reset at the start of the plan year. A quote given before the reset is not honoured after it.
Insurance · Vision plan or medical: which one bills

The reason the patient gives on the phone decides which benefit the visit runs on.

  • Routine, vision plan. Due for glasses or an annual check, no complaint.
  • Medical. Names a symptom: red eye, pain, flashes, a sudden change. Even if they also want glasses.
  • Both in one visit. The doctor decides which one bills. Do not promise the patient it will be the plan.
Exception: a patient with a medical diagnosis on file who calls for a routine check still bills medical if the doctor addresses it. Say the plan may not apply before they come in.
Front desk · New patient paperwork

What has to be in hand before the patient goes back.

  • Three things. Intake form, signed consent, and a photo of both sides of the card.
  • Send at booking, not on the morning of the visit.
  • Arrived with nothing? They still get seen. Short form now, the rest at checkout.
Exception: if the name on the card does not match the name on the form, fix it before the visit. Fixing it afterwards is a rebill.
