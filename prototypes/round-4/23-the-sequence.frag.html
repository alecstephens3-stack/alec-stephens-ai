Billing

Refraction: collect or bill

The vision plan covers the refraction only when the visit bills as a routine exam.
  • Routine exam on the vision plan. The refraction goes through the plan. Nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit. The refraction is not covered. Say so at check in, not at checkout.
  • Exception. If the doctor changes the visit type after the exam, the answer changes with it. Check the encounter before you take payment.
Triage

Flashes and floaters since this morning

This is the call that decides the day, and it is the one the desk forgets to ask about when the phones are stacked.
  • Ask first. Is there a curtain, a shadow, or lost vision.
  • If yes. Seen today. Say so on the call and hold the slot.
  • Exception. Do not offer a routine slot to make the schedule look tidy. Move the routine visit instead.
Scheduling

Which doctor can see them

The answer depends on the visit type, not on who has the next open slot.
  • Urgent. Any doctor on today's schedule.
  • Contact lens fitting. Book with a doctor who fits that lens type, and quote the fitting fee on the call.
  • Exception. A medical follow up goes back to the doctor who saw them, even if that is a longer wait.
Insurance

Vision plan or medical

The complaint decides the billing, and the patient hears the answer before the visit.
  • Routine. No complaint, due for a check. Vision plan.
  • Medical. A symptom or a diagnosis being followed. Medical, with the medical copay.
  • Exception. A patient can carry both. Bill the reason for the visit, and tell them at check in which one is being used.
Prices

This year's price

One page, edited by the office manager, dated. Nobody quotes last year's number off a sticky note.
  • Quote from the page. Not from memory, and not from the old printout in the drawer.
  • Say what is included. The patient asks about the total, not the line item.
  • Exception. If the fee schedule changed mid year, the page carries both dates and which one applies.
Front desk

New patient with no card at check in

The visit does not stop. The verification does.
  • Take the details. Carrier, member name, date of birth. That is usually enough to verify.
  • If it does not verify. Tell them before the exam what they may owe, and take payment at checkout.
  • Exception. Never guess a benefit out loud. A quoted benefit that turns out wrong becomes a refund and a phone call.
