Billing · situation page

Refraction: collect or bill

The vision plan covers the refraction only when the visit bills as a routine exam.

  • Routine on the vision plan. It goes through the plan. Nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit. Not a covered service. Say so at check in, not at checkout.
  • Visit type changed after the exam. The answer changes with it. Check before you take payment.
Exception

If the visit bills medical, the refraction is not covered. Collect it at checkout, and tell the patient at check in so it is not a surprise.

Insurance · situation page

Vision plan or medical: which one bills

The reason the patient gives on the phone decides which benefit the visit runs on.

  • Due for glasses, no complaint. Routine, vision plan.
  • Names a symptom. Red eye, pain, flashes, a sudden change: medical, even if they also want glasses.
  • Both in one visit. The doctor decides which one bills. Do not promise the patient it will be the vision plan.
Exception

A patient with a medical diagnosis already on file who calls for a routine check still bills medical if the doctor addresses that diagnosis. Say the plan may not apply before they come in.

Scheduling · situation page

Same day or next available: phone triage

The short list of what cannot wait for the next open routine slot.

  • Flashes, new floaters, a curtain or a shadow. Same day, and tell the doctor on duty.
  • Chemical splash or something in the eye. Same day, and start them rinsing while they are still on the phone.
  • Red, painful eye in a contact lens wearer. Same day. Gradual blur with no pain goes to next available.
Exception

If nothing is open, do not send them away. Take the message to the doctor on duty and call the patient back with an answer the same day.

Scheduling · situation page

Which doctor can see this patient

Not every visit type fits every provider's schedule. Check before you book, not after.

  • Young children and first exams. Only the providers taking them, and the lookup shows who is taking them this week.
  • Specialty fittings and post op follow ups. Back to the provider who started the case.
  • Surgery days. Some providers do not take walk in medical visits on them.
Exception

If the only opening is with a provider who does not take that visit type, book the right provider on a later day. A visit that has to be redone costs more than the wait.

Optical · situation page

Contact lens follow up: what is included

Follow ups inside the fitting period are part of the fitting fee. After it closes they are a new charge.

  • Recheck inside the period. No new charge. Book it as a follow up, not a new exam.
  • New brand or power after it closes. New fitting.
  • Still has trials. Ask the doctor before you order anything.
Exception

A patient who never came back for the recheck is still inside the fitting period until it closes. Do not charge them again to finish what they already paid for.

Recall · situation page

Recall cards: what to say when they call back

Patients call and ask why they got a card. Answer from the reason, not from a guess.

  • Routine. They are due for their annual exam. Book it.
  • Medical. The doctor asked to see them again for something specific. Say the doctor asked, then book it.
  • Scleral lens wearers. Their own letter, and those patients usually already know why.
Exception

If the patient says they were already seen, do not argue and do not rebook. Take the note to the office manager so the record gets corrected and the cards stop.

Questions

The six you would ask on the call.

Open the ones you care about.
  • Do you hold our patient data?

    It differs per product, so here it is per product.

    • Front desk knowledge base. None. It holds your rules, not your charts, and there are 0 patient records in it.
    • ReExam. Yes, patient contact details. A BAA is signed before any data moves, the data stays in your own systems, and our inboxes hold counts and a link, never patient names.
    • Practice operations. Staff data, not patient data. Time off and payroll only.
  • Does it connect to our practice management system?

    No, and that is on purpose, so it does not matter which one you run. ReExam works from a list your own team exports, or from a limited login you create and can revoke on any afternoon you like.

  • We already pay for engagement software. Do we rip it out?

    No. We run alongside it. Keep your phone system, we will work the list it ignores, and opt outs propagate both ways.

  • Who answers when something breaks?

    Both founders, and both are based in Asia. Urgent issues are answered by the next business morning, US time. One ReExam operator works from South Korea, which HIPAA permits and we say up front rather than when asked.

  • What are we locked into?

    Month to month, no term. Keep every page on cancellation and export any time. ReExam cancels monthly with no setup fee.

  • How long until it is live?

    Knowledge base, 30 days. ReExam, 10 business days from kickoff, and the guarantee clock starts at the first send.
