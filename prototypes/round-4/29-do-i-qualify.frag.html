Scheduling · Decision lookup

How soon do they need to be seen

  • Same day. Sudden flashes, new floaters, a curtain or a shadow in the vision. Tell the doctor on duty.
  • Same day. Chemical splash or something in the eye. Start them rinsing while they are still on the phone.
  • Next available. Gradual blur, no pain, no redness.

Exception. If nothing is open, do not send them away. Take the message to the doctor on duty and call the patient back with an answer the same day.

Scheduling · Decision lookup

Which doctor can see them

  • Young children and first exams. Only the providers taking them this week. The lookup names them.
  • Specialty fittings and post op. Back to the provider who started the case.
  • Surgery days. Some providers take no walk in medical visits.

Exception. If the only opening is with a provider who does not take that visit type, book the right provider on a later day. A visit that has to be redone costs more than the wait.

Billing · Decision lookup

Collect or bill the refraction

  • Routine on the vision plan. The refraction goes through the plan. Nothing to collect at the desk.
  • Medical visit. Not a covered service. Say so at check in, not at checkout.
  • Visit type changed after the exam. The answer changes with it. Check the encounter before you take payment.

Exception. A patient told at check in that the refraction is not covered still gets told again at checkout. Surprises at the counter are what generate the call the next morning.

Billing · Decision lookup

This year's price

  • One current list. If a printed sheet disagrees with this page, this page wins.
  • Ask which one applies. Self pay and plan prices are different.
  • Not on the page. Say you will confirm and call back. Do not estimate.

Exception. Prices reset at the start of the plan year. A quote given before the reset is not honoured after it, so check the page again when the year turns.

Insurance

Vision plan or medical

  • Routine, vision plan. Due for glasses or an annual check, no complaint.
  • Medical. Names a symptom, red eye, pain, flashes, a sudden change. Even if they also want glasses.
  • Both in one visit. The doctor decides which one bills. Do not promise the vision plan.

Exception. A patient with a medical diagnosis already on file who calls for a routine check still bills medical if the doctor addresses it. Say the plan may not apply before they come in.

Optical

Contact lens follow up

  • Inside the fitting period. No new charge. Book it as a follow up, not a new exam.
  • New brand or power after it closes. New fitting.
  • Still has trials and wants a change. Ask the doctor before you order anything.

Exception. A patient who never came back is still inside the fitting period until it closes. Do not charge them again to finish what they already paid for.

Front desk

New patient paperwork

  • Before they go back. Intake form, signed consent, a photo of both sides of the card.
  • Send it at booking. Not on the morning of the visit.
  • Arrived with nothing. They still get seen. Short form now, the rest at checkout.

Exception. If the name on the card does not match the name on the form, stop and fix it before the visit. Fixing it afterwards is a rebill.

Recall

Why did I get this card

  • Routine recall. They are due for their annual exam. Book it.
  • Medical recall. The doctor asked to see them again for something specific. Say that, then book it.
  • Scleral lens wearers. Their own letter, and those patients usually already know why.

Exception. If the patient says they were already seen, do not argue and do not rebook. Take the note to the office manager so the record gets corrected and the cards stop.

Per product. The knowledge base holds 0 patient records. ReExam touches patient contact details, so a BAA is signed before any data moves, the data stays in your own systems, and our inboxes hold counts and a link, never patient names.

No, on purpose. We work from an export your team runs, or a limited login you create and can revoke. Nothing writes back into charts.
